Unit 1 of 6 · Core Concept

Thrust Belongs to a Chosen Interface, Not to an Isolated Force Arrow

R = √(N² + f²)
⚡ The Crucial Inferential Step

Thrust at a solid interface is the normal (perpendicular) component of the contact interaction on the chosen body. A rough surface may simultaneously exert tangential friction; the vector sum of those components is the total contact reaction, not the thrust alone.

Figure 1: Decomposition of surface contact interaction into normal thrust N and tangential friction f.

Construction Steps & Theoretical Validation

1
Identify Chosen Body & Contact Interface Force ownership is body-specific. Always define the body before drawing interaction arrows to prevent mixing Newton's 3rd-law pairs.
2
Resolve Interface into Perpendicular & Tangential Components Decompose contact reaction into normal component N (thrust) and tangential component f (friction). These directions are mutually orthogonal.
3
Distinguish Normal Thrust from Total Reaction Thrust is strictly the normal component N. The total reaction force R is the vector sum R = √(N² + f²).
4
Verify Third-Law Partner Reactions The equal-and-opposite reaction force acts on the table, not on the chosen body. Check: N_partner = -N on table.
🏆 Official Olympiad Anchor: INJSO 2016 · Q32A

Problem: Two cubical boxes P (4 kg) and Q (8 kg) are in contact on a rough horizontal table. Their kinetic friction coefficients are μ_P = 0.4 and μ_Q = 0.6. A horizontal force F pushes P so that both move at constant speed. Take g = 10 m/s². Find: (i) F, and (ii) the magnitude and direction of the resultant force exerted by the table on Q.

Step-by-step Solution:
1. Vertical equilibrium on Q: N_Q = m_Q · g = 8 × 10 = 80 N (upward normal thrust).
2. Kinetic friction on Q: f_Q = μ_Q · N_Q = 0.6 × 80 = 48 N (backward).
3. Total table reaction on Q: R_Q = √(N_Q² + f_Q²) = √(80² + 48²) = √(6400 + 2304) = √8704 ≈ 93.3 N.
4. Angle of reaction: θ = arctan(N_Q / f_Q) = arctan(80 / 48) = arctan(5/3) ≈ 59.0° above horizontal.
⚠️ Common Trap & Error Prevention

Trap: Assuming the table's total force on the sliding box is simply 80 N.
Repair: 80 N is only the normal thrust component. When friction exists, the table exerts a 2D vector force whose resultant magnitude is √(N² + f²) = 93.3 N.

Unit 2 of 6 · Dynamics

N = mg is a Result with Conditions, Not the Definition of Thrust

ΣF⊥ = m · a⊥ ⇒ N = m(g ± a⊥) ∓ F_ext,⊥
⚡ The Crucial Inferential Step

A normal force is a constraint response determined by the full perpendicular equation ΣF⊥ = m·a⊥. N = mg appears only for a horizontal surface with no other vertical forces and zero vertical acceleration. When an external pull acts at angle θ, N = mg - F·sinθ.

Perpendicular Dynamics & Contact Loss Threshold

1
Choose the Normal Coordinate Axis Assign a positive perpendicular direction normal to the contact interface before resolving forces.
2
Sum All Perpendicular External Forces Include the normal thrust N, gravitational weight component mg·cosα, and any external applied force components F·sinθ.
3
Apply Newton's Second Law: ΣF⊥ = m · a⊥ For static equilibrium (a⊥ = 0): N + F·sinθ - mg = 0 ⇒ N = mg - F·sinθ.
4
Enforce Unilateral Contact Constraint (N ≥ 0) Surfaces can only push, never pull. If algebra requires N < 0, the body lifts off the surface, breaking contact (N = 0).
⚠️ Common Trap & Error Prevention

Trap: Setting N = mg by default when a block is pulled obliquely upward.
Repair: The vertical component of the pull F·sinθ shares the upward load, reducing the required normal reaction: N = mg - F·sinθ < mg.

Unit 3 of 6 · Definition

Average Pressure Depends on Total Normal Thrust and Actual Loaded Area

P_avg = F⊥ / A_actual = N / A
⚡ The Crucial Inferential Step

Pressure is defined strictly as the ratio of perpendicular compressive thrust to the actual contact area. SI unit: Pascal (1 Pa = 1 N/m²). Force is a vector with direction; pressure in a static fluid is a scalar quantity acting perpendicularly inward on every submerged surface.

🏆 Benchmark Example: INJSO 2013 · Q58 Contact Pressure

Problem: A solid metal cylinder of mass M = 15 kg and radius r = 5 cm stands vertically on a flat horizontal floor. Take g = 9.8 m/s². Calculate the average pressure exerted on the floor.

Solution:
1. Normal thrust: N = M · g = 15 × 9.8 = 147 N.
2. Contact area: A = π · r² = π · (0.05 m)² = 0.007854 m².
3. Average pressure: P = N / A = 147 / 0.007854 = 18,716 Pa ≈ 18.7 kPa.
Unit 4 of 6 · Dimensional Analysis

Similar Objects Make Weight and Contact Area Scale with Different Powers

Mass ∝ L³, Area ∝ L² ⇒ Pressure ∝ L
⚡ The Crucial Inferential Step

When an object is scaled up by a linear factor λ: volume and weight increase by λ³ (cube power), while contact area increases only by λ² (square power). Consequently, pressure scales linearly with size: P ∝ λ. Larger geometrically similar animals or structures exert substantially higher ground pressure!

🏆 Official Olympiad Anchor: INJSO 2012 · Q39 Scaling Proof

Problem: Cube 1 has side length L and exerts pressure P₁ on the floor. Cube 2 is made of the same uniform material but has side length 2L. Determine the ratio of pressure P₂ / P₁.

Dimensional Derivation:
• Weight W₁ = ρ · L³ · g, Base Area A₁ = L² ⇒ P₁ = W₁ / A₁ = ρ · g · L.
• Weight W₂ = ρ · (2L)³ · g = 8 · W₁, Base Area A₂ = (2L)² = 4 · A₁.
• Pressure P₂ = W₂ / A₂ = 8·W₁ / (4·A₁) = 2 · P₁.
• Therefore: P₂ / P₁ = 2. The pressure directly doubles.
Unit 5 of 6 · Vector Geometry

Pressure is Scalar; The Surface Normal Gives the Force Direction

dF_vector = P · dA · n_hat
⚡ The Crucial Inferential Step

Pressure has no intrinsic direction: it is an isotropic scalar state within a fluid. The vector direction of the resulting hydrostatic force is entirely defined by the outward normal unit vector n_hat of the contacting surface.

🏆 Official Olympiad Anchor: INJSO 2013 · Q62 Tilted Surface Analysis

Problem: A rectangular hatch of area A is inclined at angle θ to the horizontal at average depth h in water of density ρ. Find the magnitude and direction of the hydrostatic force on the hatch.

Derivation:
• Scalar hydrostatic pressure at depth h: P = ρ · g · h.
• Total normal force magnitude: F = P · A = ρ · g · h · A.
• Direction: Perpendicular to the hatch face (at angle 90° - θ to horizontal).
Unit 6 of 6 · Fluid Statics & Thermodynamics

Depth Adds Gauge Pressure; Gas Laws Need Absolute Pressure

P_abs = P_atm + ρ·g·h  |  P₁V₁ = P₂V₂
⚡ The Crucial Inferential Step

At depth h in a liquid with free surface exposed to atmospheric pressure P_atm: Gauge pressure = ρ·g·h, while Absolute pressure P_abs = P_atm + ρ·g·h. Whenever hydrostatics is coupled to the ideal gas law (e.g. rising air bubbles), you must use absolute pressure P_abs. Substituting gauge pressure creates complete algebraic failure.

💡 Classic Application: Air Bubble Rising from Lake Bottom

Problem: An air bubble of volume V₁ forms at depth h = 20 m in fresh water (ρ = 1000 kg/m³, g = 10 m/s²). Atmospheric pressure P_atm = 10⁵ Pa. Assuming isothermal conditions, find its volume V₂ just as it reaches the surface.

Solution:
1. Gauge pressure at depth: P_gauge = ρ · g · h = 1000 × 10 × 20 = 2 × 10⁵ Pa.
2. Absolute pressure at depth: P₁ = P_atm + P_gauge = 10⁵ + 2 × 10⁵ = 3 × 10⁵ Pa.
3. Absolute pressure at surface: P₂ = P_atm = 1 × 10⁵ Pa.
4. Isothermal Boyle's Law: P₁ · V₁ = P₂ · V₂ ⇒ V₂ = (P₁ / P₂) · V₁ = (3 × 10⁵ / 10⁵) · V₁ = 3 · V₁.
The bubble volume triples.
⚠️ Common Trap & Error Prevention

Trap: Using P₁ = ρ·g·h = 2 × 10⁵ Pa without adding atmospheric pressure P_atm.
Repair: Gas volume depends on the total collisions with the bubble wall (absolute pressure). Never omit P_atm when applying Boyle's Law!
