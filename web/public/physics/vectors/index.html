Unit 1 of 6 · Geometric Foundation

Scalars, Vectors & Geometric Translation Invariance

A = |A| · u_A  |  Parallel Transport Preserves Invariance
⚡ The Crucial Inferential Step

A physical quantity is a vector only if it possesses magnitude, a specified directional orientation, AND obeys the parallelogram law of vector addition. (Quantities like electric current have direction, but add algebraically, so they are scalars!). A free vector in Euclidean space is completely invariant under parallel translation (shifting position without rotating preserves the vector).

1
The Three Vector Tests 1. Has real positive magnitude.
2. Has unambiguous spatial direction.
3. Obeys the triangle / parallelogram addition law.
2
Unit Vector Definition A vector of magnitude 1 pointing along A: u_A = A / |A|. Any vector is its magnitude times its unit vector: A = |A| u_A.
💡 Concept Counter-Example: Is Electric Current a Vector?

Question: Current has both magnitude (e.g. 5 A) and direction (along a copper wire). Why is electric current classified as a scalar rather than a vector?

Answer:
If two wires meet at an angle θ carrying 3 A and 4 A, the combined junction current is ALWAYS 3 + 4 = 7 A (Kirchhoff's Current Law), regardless of whether the wire angle is 30°, 90°, or 180°! If current were a vector, the junction current at 90° would be √(3² + 4²) = 5 A. Because current does not obey vector addition, it is a scalar.
⚠️ Common Trap & Error Prevention

Trap: Assuming that "having a direction" is sufficient to make a physical quantity a vector.
Repair: The defining test of a vector is adherence to the vector addition law under coordinate rotations.

Unit 2 of 6 · Composition of Vectors

Vector Addition: Triangle & Parallelogram Laws

R = √(A² + B² + 2AB cosθ)  |  tan α = (B sinθ) / (A + B cosθ)
⚡ The Crucial Inferential Step

The resultant magnitude of two vectors A and B acting at angle θ ranges between |A - B| (when antiparallel, θ = 180°) and A + B (when collinear, θ = 0°). Direct algebraic sum (A + B) is valid ONLY when θ = 0°. For perpendicular vectors (θ = 90°), the formula reduces directly to the Pythagorean theorem: R = √(A² + B²).

💡 Worked Problem: Resultant of Equal Forces at 120°

Problem: Two forces of equal magnitude F act at an angle θ = 120° to each other. Find the magnitude and direction of their resultant.

Solution:
1. Apply resultant formula: R = √(F² + F² + 2·F·F·cos 120°).
2. Since cos 120° = -0.5: R = √(2F² + 2F²(-0.5)) = √(2F² - F²) = √F² = F.
3. Direction: tan α = (F sin 120°) / (F + F cos 120°) = (F · √3/2) / (F - F/2) = (√3/2) / (1/2) = √3 ⇒ α = 60°.
The resultant of two equal forces at 120° has magnitude EXACTLY EQUAL to each force, bisecting the angle!

⚠️ Common Trap & Error Prevention

Trap: Adding vector magnitudes algebraically (e.g. 5 N + 5 N = 10 N) regardless of angle.
Repair: 5 N + 5 N can produce any value from 0 N (at 180°) to 10 N (at 0°). Always use the cosine addition law.

Unit 3 of 6 · Cartesian Analysis

Cartesian Resolution & Orthogonal Unit Vectors

A = A_x î + A_y ĵ + A_z k̂  |  A_x = A cosθ, A_y = A sinθ
⚡ The Crucial Inferential Step

Any vector in 2D or 3D can be uniquely decomposed into mutually perpendicular projections along the standard Cartesian unit vectors (î, ĵ, k̂). To add N arbitrary vectors, simply resolve each vector into components, sum the components independently (R_x = Σ A_ix, R_y = Σ A_iy), and reassemble the final resultant magnitude via Pythagoras: |R| = √(R_x² + R_y²).

1
Trigonometric Projections Adjacent component = Magnitude × cos(angle). Opposite component = Magnitude × sin(angle).
2
Component-Wise Addition Rule A + B = (A_x + B_x) î + (A_y + B_y) ĵ. This reduces complex multi-angle geometry into elementary arithmetic.
3
Angle Recovery with Quadrant Awareness tan θ = R_y / R_x. Check the signs of R_x and R_y to position θ in the correct Cartesian quadrant (I, II, III, or IV).
💡 Worked Problem: Resultant of Three Concurrent Forces

Problem: Three forces act on a particle: F₁ = 10 N along +x axis, F₂ = 20 N along +y axis, F₃ = 10 N along -x axis. Find the net resultant force.

Solution:
1. Resolve into components:
• F₁ = 10 î + 0 ĵ.
• F₂ = 0 î + 20 ĵ.
• F₃ = -10 î + 0 ĵ.
2. Sum components: R_x = 10 + 0 - 10 = 0 N; R_y = 0 + 20 + 0 = 20 N.
3. Reassemble: R = 0 î + 20 ĵ ⇒ |R| = 20 N, pointing strictly along +y direction (90°).
⚠️ Common Trap & Error Prevention

Trap: Blindly using cos for the horizontal axis when the problem gives the angle relative to the vertical axis.
Repair: The cosine component ALWAYS belongs to the axis adjacent to the reference angle θ.

Unit 4 of 6 · Kinematic Transformations

Vector Subtraction & Change in State Vectors

A - B = A + (-B)  |  |Δv| = 2v sin(Δθ / 2)  (UCM)
⚡ The Crucial Inferential Step

Vector subtraction is defined as the addition of the negative vector: A - B = A + (-B). In physics, acceleration is defined by change in velocity: a = Δv / Δt = (v_final - v_initial) / Δt. Even if speed is constant, rotating a velocity vector produces a non-zero subtraction difference vector Δv directed inward!

1
Geometric Construction of -B Reverse the arrowhead of vector B by exactly 180° while keeping its length unchanged. Add A and -B head-to-tail.
2
Change in Velocity for Equal Speed v at Angle θ |Δv| = √(v² + v² - 2v² cosθ) = √(2v²(1 - cosθ)) = √(2v² · 2 sin²(θ/2)) = 2v sin(θ/2).
💡 Worked Problem: Car Turning a Right-Angle Corner

Problem: A car travels east at speed v = 20 m/s and turns north onto a crossroad, continuing at the same speed v = 20 m/s. Calculate the change in velocity vector Δv and its direction.

Solution:
1. Initial velocity: v_i = +20 î m/s (East).
2. Final velocity: v_f = +20 ĵ m/s (North).
3. Change in velocity: Δv = v_f - v_i = -20 î + 20 ĵ m/s.
4. Magnitude: |Δv| = √((-20)² + 20²) = √(400 + 400) = √800 = 28.28 m/s.
5. Direction: tan θ = 20 / (-20) = -1 ⇒ North-West (135° from East).
Notice: Even though speed did not change by a single m/s, the velocity change is 28.28 m/s!
⚠️ Common Trap & Error Prevention

Trap: Claiming Δv = 0 because speed remained 20 m/s before and after the turn.
Repair: Speed is a scalar (|v|); velocity is a vector. Direction change requires non-zero acceleration.

Unit 5 of 6 · Energy & Projections

Scalar (Dot) Product: Geometric Projections & Work Done

A · B = |A||B| cosθ = A_x B_x + A_y B_y + A_z B_z  |  A ⊥ B ⇔ A · B = 0
⚡ The Crucial Inferential Step

The scalar (dot) product multiplies the magnitude of one vector by the collinear projection of the second vector onto the first: A · B = A (B cosθ). The result is a pure SCALAR. In physics, Work is the dot product of force and displacement: W = F · d. If force is perpendicular to displacement (θ = 90°), the work done is STRICTLY ZERO.

1
Orthonormal Basis Identities î · î = ĵ · ĵ = k̂ · k̂ = 1. Cross-terms: î · ĵ = ĵ · k̂ = k̂ · î = 0.
2
Orthogonality Test Two non-zero vectors A and B are perpendicular if and only if their dot product vanishes: A · B = 0.
3
Scalar Projection Formula Projection of B onto A = B cosθ = (A · B) / |A|.
💡 Worked Problem: Work Done by a 3D Force

Problem: A force F = (3 î + 4 ĵ - 2 k̂) N displaces an object from point P(1, 2, 0) m to point Q(4, 6, 2) m. Evaluate the work done by the force and determine the angle between force and displacement.

Solution:
1. Displacement vector: d = (4 - 1) î + (6 - 2) ĵ + (2 - 0) k̂ = 3 î + 4 ĵ + 2 k̂ m.
2. Work done: W = F · d = (3)(3) + (4)(4) + (-2)(2) = 9 + 16 - 4 = 21.00 J.
3. Magnitudes: |F| = √(3² + 4² + (-2)²) = √(9 + 16 + 4) = √29 ≈ 5.385 N.
|d| = √(3² + 4² + 2²) = √(9 + 16 + 4) = √29 ≈ 5.385 m.
4. Angle: cos θ = (F · d) / (|F||d|) = 21 / (√29 × √29) = 21 / 29 ≈ 0.7241 ⇒ θ = 43.6°.
⚠️ Common Trap & Error Prevention

Trap: Assigning a unit vector direction (î, ĵ, k̂) to the output of a dot product.
Repair: The dot product is a SCALAR; its result has magnitude and sign, but zero spatial direction.

Unit 6 of 6 · Rotational Vectors

Vector (Cross) Product: Torque & Right-Hand Rules

A × B = (|A||B| sinθ) n̂  |  A × B = -(B × A)  |  τ = r × F
⚡ The Crucial Inferential Step

The vector (cross) product produces a new vector strictly perpendicular to the plane containing A and B, whose orientation is determined by the Right-Hand Screw Rule. Its magnitude equals the geometric area of the parallelogram spanned by A and B: |A × B| = AB sinθ. Unlike regular multiplication, the cross product is anti-commutative: A × B = -(B × A)!

1
Right-Hand Screw Rule Point right-hand fingers along vector A and curl them toward vector B through the smaller angle θ. Your extended thumb points along the unit normal vector n̂.
2
Collinear & Parallel Vectors Vanish If A and B are parallel or antiparallel (θ = 0° or 180°), sin θ = 0 ⇒ A × B = 0. In particular, A × A = 0.
3
Determinant Matrix Evaluation A × B = det [î, ĵ, k̂; A_x, A_y, A_z; B_x, B_y, B_z] = î(A_y B_z - A_z B_y) - ĵ(A_x B_z - A_z B_x) + k̂(A_x B_y - A_y B_x).
💡 Worked Problem: Rotational Torque on a Wrench

Problem: A position vector r = (0.4 î + 0.2 ĵ) m locates a bolt. A force F = (-20 î + 50 ĵ) N is applied at the end of the wrench. Evaluate the torque vector τ = r × F acting about the origin.

Solution:
1. Apply 2D cross product: r × F = (r_x F_y - r_y F_x) k̂.
2. r_x F_y = (0.4) × (50) = +20 N·m.
3. r_y F_x = (0.2) × (-20) = -4 N·m.
4. τ = (20 - (-4)) k̂ = +24 k̂ N·m.
The torque vector has magnitude 24 N·m pointing strictly out of the page (+z direction, counter-clockwise rotation).
⚠️ Common Trap & Error Prevention

Trap: Commuting the order of terms: writing r × F = F × r.
Repair: Swapping the cross-product operands reverses the vector direction: F × r = -24 k̂ N·m (clockwise). Order matters strictly.
