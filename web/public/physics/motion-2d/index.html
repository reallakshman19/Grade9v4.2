Unit 1 of 6 · Kinematic Decomposition

Orthogonal Independence & The Shared Clock

x(t) = (u cosθ)t  |  y(t) = (u sinθ)t - ½gt²
⚡ The Crucial Inferential Step

In projectile motion, horizontal motion (x) and vertical motion (y) are completely uncoupled, independent one-dimensional motions. The horizontal acceleration is strictly zero (a_x = 0), while vertical acceleration is constant gravitational free fall (a_y = -g). They are linked solely by one scalar parameter: time t (the shared clock).

1
Horizontal Kinematic Channel (No Force) Σ F_x = 0 ⇒ a_x = 0. Velocity is invariant: v_x(t) = u cosθ. Position: x(t) = (u cosθ)t.
2
Vertical Kinematic Channel (Uniform Gravity) Σ F_y = -mg ⇒ a_y = -g. Velocity: v_y(t) = u sinθ - gt. Position: y(t) = (u sinθ)t - ½gt².
3
The Shared Parameter Solve t from the constraint of one channel and substitute into the other channel.
💡 The Bullet Paradox: Dropped vs. Fired Horizontally

Problem: A high-velocity bullet is fired horizontally at 500 m/s from a height of 19.6 m above level ground. At the exact same microsecond, a second bullet is dropped from rest from the same height. Which bullet strikes the ground first?

Solution:
1. Vertical motion equation for both bullets: y = ½gt² (since initial vertical velocity u_y = 0 for both).
2. Time to reach ground: t = √(2h / g) = √(2 × 19.6 / 9.8) = √4 = 2.00 s.
3. Both bullets strike the ground at the EXACT same instant! The fired bullet travels 500 × 2 = 1000 m horizontally, but its vertical descent is completely unaffected by its horizontal speed.
⚠️ Common Trap & Error Prevention

Trap: Believing that horizontal velocity "carries" or lifts the projectile, delaying its landing time.
Repair: Gravity acts only vertically and is blind to horizontal motion. Orthogonal independence is strict and absolute.

Unit 2 of 6 · Canonical Invariants

The Three Ground-to-Ground Projectile Invariants

T = 2u sinθ / g  |  H_max = u² sin²θ / (2g)  |  R = u² sin 2θ / g
⚡ The Crucial Inferential Step

For ground-to-ground level flight, all three canonical metrics derive directly from the vertical channel: Time of Flight (T) comes from y = 0; Maximum Height (H_max) comes from v_y = 0; and Horizontal Range (R) is the horizontal distance covered during total flight time T: R = u_x · T.

1
Time of Flight T Set y = 0 ⇒ t(u sinθ - ½gt) = 0 ⇒ T = (2u sinθ)/g. Note: t_apex = T/2 = (u sinθ)/g.
2
Maximum Height H_max At peak, v_y = 0 ⇒ 0 = (u sinθ)² - 2g H_max ⇒ H_max = (u² sin²θ)/(2g).
3
Horizontal Range R R = u_x · T = (u cosθ) · (2u sinθ / g) = u² (2 sinθ cosθ) / g = u² sin(2θ) / g.
4
Maximum Range Condition sin(2θ) reaches its mathematical maximum of 1 when 2θ = 90° ⇒ θ = 45°. R_max = u²/g = 4 H_max.
💡 Worked Problem: The 45° Range-to-Height Relation

Problem: Prove that when a projectile is launched at θ = 45° to achieve maximum range, its maximum height is exactly one-fourth of the range: R_max = 4 H_max.

Solution:
1. At θ = 45°: R_max = u² sin(90°) / g = u²/g.
2. At θ = 45°: H_max = u² sin²(45°) / (2g) = u² (1/√2)² / (2g) = u²(1/2)/(2g) = u² / (4g).
3. Dividing: R_max / H_max = (u²/g) / (u² / 4g) = 4.
Therefore: R = 4 H cotθ for any launch angle θ! At θ = 45°, cot(45°) = 1 ⇒ R = 4H.
⚠️ The Apex Fallacy

Trap: Stating that velocity is zero at the highest point of projectile flight.
Repair: Only the VERTICAL velocity component v_y vanishes! The horizontal component v_x = u cosθ is at full speed. Net velocity at apex is (u cosθ) î, NEVER zero.

Unit 3 of 6 · Trajectory Geometry

Trajectory Parabola & Complementary Angle Invariance

y = x tanθ - gx²/(2u² cos²θ)  |  R(θ) = R(90° - θ)
⚡ The Crucial Inferential Step

Eliminating time t = x / (u cosθ) reveals that the spatial path y(x) is an inverted Cartesian parabola of the form y = Ax - Bx². Furthermore, any two complementary launch angles θ and (90° - θ) produce the EXACT same horizontal range R for the same initial speed u!

1
Trajectory Equation Derivation From x = (u cosθ)t ⇒ t = x / (u cosθ). Substitute into y = (u sinθ)t - ½gt² ⇒ y = x tanθ - [g / (2u² cos²θ)] x².
2
Range Form of Trajectory Factor out x tanθ: y = x tanθ [1 - x / R], where R = u² sin 2θ / g.
3
Proof of Complementary Angle Range Equality Replace θ with (90° - θ): R(90° - θ) = u² sin(2(90° - θ)) / g = u² sin(180° - 2θ) / g. Since sin(180° - φ) = sin φ, R(90° - θ) = u² sin 2θ / g = R(θ).
💡 Classic Olympiad Problem: Flight Times of Complementary Arcs

Problem: Two projectiles are fired with the same speed u at complementary angles θ₁ = θ and θ₂ = 90° - θ, covering the same range R. Let their times of flight be T₁ and T₂. Find the relationship between T₁·T₂ and R.

Solution:
1. T₁ = 2u sinθ / g.
2. T₂ = 2u sin(90° - θ) / g = 2u cosθ / g.
3. Multiply: T₁ · T₂ = (2u sinθ / g) × (2u cosθ / g) = (2/g) × [u² (2 sinθ cosθ) / g] = (2/g) · R.
Therefore: R = ½ g T₁ T₂ (Independent of launch velocity and angle!).
⚠️ Common Trap & Error Prevention

Trap: Assuming the complementary paths have equal heights or flight times.
Repair: Only the range R is equal! The steeper angle (e.g. 60° vs 30°) reaches a much higher peak (H₂ / H₁ = tan²θ) and stays in the air longer (T₂ / T₁ = tanθ).

Unit 4 of 6 · Asymmetric Flight

Elevated Clifftop Launches & Impact Telemetry

y(t) = h + (u sinθ)t - ½gt² = 0  |  v_impact = √(u² + 2gh)
⚡ The Crucial Inferential Step

When launched from an elevated cliff of height h above ground, landing occurs below the launch plane (y = -h). The canonical range formula fails completely. Flight time must be solved via the quadratic formula. By Conservation of Mechanical Energy, the final impact speed is v = √(u² + 2gh), completely invariant of launch angle θ!

1
Vertical Quadratic for Time of Flight Take launch position as origin (0, 0). Ground level is at y = -h. Equation: -h = (u sinθ)t - ½gt² ⇒ ½gt² - (u sinθ)t - h = 0.
2
Horizontal Drift Range Horizontal velocity is constant: x_drift = (u cosθ) · t_land.
3
Impact Velocity & Direction Angle v_x = u cosθ, v_y = u sinθ - g t_land. Impact angle below horizontal: tan φ = |v_y| / v_x. Speed: v = √(v_x² + v_y²) = √(u² + 2gh).
💡 Worked Problem: Horizontal Cliff Launch

Problem: A rock is kicked horizontally off a clifftop of height h = 45 m with initial speed u = 20 m/s (g = 10 m/s²). Find (a) time of flight, (b) horizontal drift, and (c) impact speed and angle.

Solution:
1. Initial vertical velocity u_y = 0 ⇒ 45 = ½(10)t² ⇒ 5t² = 45 ⇒ t = 3.0 s.
2. Horizontal drift: x = u_x · t = 20 × 3 = 60 m.
3. Final velocity components: v_x = 20 m/s, v_y = 0 - 10(3) = -30 m/s.
4. Impact speed: v = √(20² + (-30)²) = √(400 + 900) = √1300 = 36.06 m/s.
5. Impact angle: tan φ = 30 / 20 = 1.5 ⇒ φ = 56.3° below horizontal.
⚠️ Common Trap & Error Prevention

Trap: Blindly applying R = u² sin 2θ / g for clifftop or trench problems.
Repair: That formula holds ONLY when launch elevation and landing elevation are identical (Δy = 0). For any height difference, use the full kinematic equations.

Unit 5 of 6 · Multi-Body Kinematics

Relative Velocity in Two Dimensions (Rain-Man & River-Boat)

v_rel = v_A - v_B  |  t_cross = d / (v_b sinθ)  |  drift = (v_r + v_b cosθ)t
⚡ The Crucial Inferential Step

Relative velocity in 2D is the vector subtraction: v_A/B = v_A - v_B. In river crossing, the time required to cross the river depends EXCLUSIVELY on the component of velocity perpendicular to the banks: t = width / v_perpendicular. Downstream water current affects only the horizontal drift, NEVER the crossing time!

1
Shortest Time River Crossing Steer perpendicular to banks (θ = 90°). Time t_min = d / v_b (minimum possible). Drift = v_river · t_min.
2
Shortest Path (Zero Drift) Crossing Resultant ground velocity must be perpendicular to bank: v_bx + v_river = 0 ⇒ v_b cosθ = -v_r ⇒ cosθ = -v_r / v_b. (Possible only if swimmer speed v_b > river speed v_r).
3
Rain-Man Umbrella Tilt Angle Rain falls at v_r ĵ. Man runs at v_m î. Relative velocity of rain to man: v_rel = -v_m î - v_r ĵ. Umbrella tilt angle from vertical: tan θ = v_m / v_r (tilted forward).
💡 Worked Problem: River Crossing Speed & Drift

Problem: A river of width d = 500 m flows east at v_r = 3 m/s. A motorboat can travel at v_b = 5 m/s in still water. (a) Find minimum time to cross. (b) Find direction to steer to land directly opposite on the other bank (zero drift).

Solution:
1. Minimum time: Head directly North across stream (θ = 90°).
t_min = d / v_b = 500 m / 5 m/s = 100 s. Downstream drift = 3 m/s × 100 s = 300 m.
2. Zero drift: Need net horizontal velocity = 0 ⇒ 5 cosθ + 3 = 0 ⇒ cosθ = -3/5 ⇒ θ = 126.87° from river flow (36.87° upstream).
Net crossing speed = √(5² - 3²) = 4 m/s. Crossing time for zero drift = 500 / 4 = 125 s.
⚠️ Common Trap & Error Prevention

Trap: Thinking river current slows down the perpendicular crossing time when steering directly across.
Repair: The crossing time is exactly the same whether the river flows at 1 m/s or 100 m/s, because orthogonal components do not interfere.

Unit 6 of 6 · Rotational Kinematics

Uniform Circular Motion: Tangential Speed & Centripetal Acceleration

v = ω·r  |  a_c = v²/r = ω²r  |  T = 2π/ω
⚡ The Crucial Inferential Step

In Uniform Circular Motion (UCM), speed |v| is strictly constant, yet acceleration is NON-ZERO! Velocity is a vector; because its direction continuously rotates tangent to the circle, the rate of change of direction produces an inward-directed centripetal acceleration a_c = v²/r. Centripetal acceleration is strictly perpendicular to velocity at every instant (hence doing zero work).

1
Angular & Linear Velocity Relation Arc length s = r θ ⇒ differentiating with respect to time: ds/dt = r (dθ/dt) ⇒ v = ω r.
2
Centripetal Acceleration Proof Position r(t) = r(cos ωt î + sin ωt ĵ). Velocity v(t) = dr/dt = ωr(-sin ωt î + cos ωt ĵ). Acceleration a(t) = dv/dt = -ω²r(cos ωt î + sin ωt ĵ) = -ω² r (directed strictly opposite radius vector, i.e. radially inward).
3
Work Done in UCM is Zero Since a_c is strictly perpendicular to instantaneous displacement (a_c · v = 0), the power P = F · v = 0. Kinetic energy is strictly conserved.
💡 Worked Problem: High-Speed Centrifuge Acceleration

Problem: A laboratory centrifuge spins test tubes in a circle of radius r = 10 cm at 6000 rpm. Determine the centripetal acceleration experienced by the sample in terms of g (take g = 9.8 m/s²).

Solution:
1. Angular speed ω = 6000 × (2π / 60) = 200π rad/s ≈ 628.32 rad/s.
2. Radius r = 0.10 m.
3. Centripetal acceleration: a_c = ω² r = (200π)² × 0.10 = 40000π² × 0.10 = 4000π² ≈ 39,478 m/s².
4. In units of g: 39,478 / 9.8 = 4,028 g!
⚠️ Common Trap & Error Prevention

Trap: Confusing constant speed with zero acceleration in circular motion.
Repair: Acceleration measures the vector rate of change of velocity (dv/dt). Even when speed is constant, rotating the direction of the vector produces centripetal acceleration.
