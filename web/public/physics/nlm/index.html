Unit 1 of 6 · Inertial Foundations

The First Law, Inertia & Reference Frames

Σ F_ext = 0 ⇔ v = constant
⚡ The Crucial Inferential Step

Motion does NOT require force. An object at rest remains at rest, and an object in motion continues at constant velocity in a straight line, unless acted upon by a non-zero net external force. Force is the agent of change in velocity (acceleration), never the maintainer of motion.

1
Galileo's Incline Thought Experiment A ball rolling down an incline and up another incline rises to the same height. If the second incline is horizontal, the ball must travel forever to reach that height.
2
Definition of Inertial Reference Frame A frame of reference in which Newton's First Law holds true without invoking pseudo-forces. Any frame moving at constant velocity relative to an inertial frame is also inertial.
3
Equilibrium Criterion Equilibrium means zero acceleration (a = 0). It does NOT mean velocity is zero! Static equilibrium (v = 0) and dynamic equilibrium (v = const) both require Σ F = 0.
💡 The Constant-Speed Elevator Problem

Problem: A person of mass m = 70 kg stands on a weighing scale inside an elevator moving upward at a constant speed of 8 m/s. What does the scale read? What if the elevator moves downward at 8 m/s?

Solution:
1. In both cases, velocity is constant ⇒ acceleration a = 0.
2. Vertical FBD: N - mg = m(a) = 0 ⇒ N = mg = 70 × 9.8 = 686 N.
3. The scale reads exactly 70 kg (686 N) whether stationary, moving up at 8 m/s, or moving down at 8 m/s. Scale reading changes ONLY when the elevator accelerates!
⚠️ Common Trap & Error Prevention

Trap: Thinking that because the elevator is moving upward, the scale must push up harder (N > mg).
Repair: If N were greater than mg, the net force would be upward, causing the elevator to accelerate upward! Constant velocity demands N = mg.

Unit 2 of 6 · Modeling Discipline

Free Body Diagrams (FBD) & System Isolation Rules

Choose Body Boundary → Draw ONLY External Forces Acting ON the Body
⚡ The Crucial Inferential Step

A Free Body Diagram (FBD) must isolate a single chosen body and include ONLY external forces exerted BY other bodies ON that chosen body. Never include forces exerted by the chosen body on its surroundings. Normal force N is strictly perpendicular to the contact interface; friction f is strictly parallel.

1
Step 1: Declare the System Boundary Isolate Body A from all tables, ropes, and adjacent bodies. Represent it as a point mass or bounded silhouette.
2
Step 2: Non-Contact Field Forces Draw gravity W = mg acting vertically downward from the center of mass.
3
Step 3: Contact Forces at Boundaries Every physical contact point creates a force: strings pull inward along length (Tension T); solid surfaces push perpendicularly inward (Normal N) and resist tangential slip (Friction f).
4
Step 4: Coordinate Resolution Align coordinate axes with acceleration (e.g. parallel and perpendicular to an inclined plane). Resolve all off-axis vectors.
💡 Worked Problem: Block on Inclined Plane

Problem: A block of mass m = 10 kg sits at rest on a rough plane inclined at θ = 30° to the horizontal. Evaluate the normal reaction N and static friction force f_s.

Solution:
1. Resolve weight mg into components: perpendicular = mg cosθ; parallel down slope = mg sinθ.
2. Perpendicular equilibrium: Σ F_perp = N - mg cos 30° = 0 ⇒ N = 10 × 9.8 × (√3/2) = 84.87 N.
3. Parallel equilibrium: Σ F_parallel = mg sin 30° - f_s = 0 ⇒ f_s = 10 × 9.8 × 0.5 = 49.00 N (up the incline).
Notice: Normal reaction N is NOT mg! N = mg cosθ.

⚠️ Common Trap & Error Prevention

Trap: Drawing "ma" as a force on an FBD, or writing N = mg automatically.
Repair: "ma" is the RESULTANT acceleration product (Σ F = ma), NOT a physical force! Only draw real physical interactions (gravity, normal, tension, friction).

Unit 3 of 6 · Dynamics & Constraints

Newton's Second Law & Atwood Pulley Constraints

Σ F = m a  |  a = (m₂ - m₁)g / (m₁ + m₂)  |  T = 2m₁m₂ g / (m₁ + m₂)
⚡ The Crucial Inferential Step

For connected bodies linked by inextensible taut strings, the geometric string constraint L = x₁ + x₂ = constant requires acceleration magnitudes to be identical: a₁ = a₂ = a. Write the Second Law equation for each body separately, then sum or substitute to eliminate the internal constraint force (Tension T).

1
Equation for Ascending Mass m₁ Tension pulls up, gravity pulls down: T - m₁ g = m₁ a.
2
Equation for Descending Mass m₂ (m₂ > m₁) Gravity pulls down, tension pulls up: m₂ g - T = m₂ a.
3
Sum to Eliminate Tension T (T - m₁ g) + (m₂ g - T) = (m₁ + m₂) a ⇒ (m₂ - m₁) g = (m₁ + m₂) a ⇒ a = (m₂ - m₁)g / (m₁ + m₂).
4
Substitute to Solve String Tension T T = m₁ (g + a) = m₁ [g + (m₂ - m₁)g / (m₁ + m₂)] = 2 m₁ m₂ g / (m₁ + m₂).
💡 Worked Problem: Standard Atwood Machine

Problem: Two masses m₁ = 3 kg and m₂ = 5 kg are suspended over a light frictionless pulley. Take g = 10 m/s². Determine the system acceleration and the tension in the string.

Solution:
1. System acceleration: a = (5 - 3) × 10 / (5 + 3) = 2 × 10 / 8 = 20 / 8 = 2.50 m/s².
2. String tension: T = 2 × 3 × 5 × 10 / (3 + 5) = 300 / 8 = 37.50 N.
3. Notice: For m₂ = 5 kg, gravity is 50 N, but tension is only 37.5 N (net downward force = 12.5 N = 5 × 2.5 N). Tension is NOT 50 N!

⚠️ Common Trap & Error Prevention

Trap: Assuming tension equals the weight of the heavier block (T = m₂ g).
Repair: If T were equal to m₂ g, block 2 would not accelerate downward! T is strictly less than m₂ g during downward acceleration.

Unit 4 of 6 · Momentum & Impacts

Momentum, Impulse & Average Impact Forces

p = m v  |  J = Δp = ∫ F dt = F_avg · Δt
⚡ The Crucial Inferential Step

Momentum is a signed vector quantity p = m v. Rebounding from a wall produces double the momentum change of stopping: Δp = m(v - (-u)) = 2mu. Impulse J equals this change in momentum. To minimize destructive impact force F_avg, the collision duration Δt must be prolonged (the fundamental engineering principle of airbags, crumple zones, and catching cricket balls with soft hands).

1
Impulse-Momentum Theorem From Newton II: F = dp/dt ⇒ F dt = dp ⇒ J = ∫ F dt = Δp = m v_final - m v_initial.
2
Average Impact Force F_avg = Δp / Δt. For a fixed momentum change, peak force is inversely proportional to collision contact time Δt.
💡 Worked Problem: Rebounding Cricket Ball Force

Problem: A cricket ball of mass m = 0.15 kg strikes a batsman's bat horizontally at u = 25 m/s and rebounds in the opposite direction at v = 25 m/s. The contact duration is Δt = 0.002 s. Find the impulse delivered and the average force exerted on the bat.

Solution:
1. Choose incoming direction as negative (-25 m/s) and rebound direction as positive (+25 m/s).
2. Δp = m(v_f - v_i) = 0.15 × (25 - (-25)) = 0.15 × 50 = +7.50 N·s.
3. Impulse J = 7.50 N·s.
4. Average force: F_avg = J / Δt = 7.50 / 0.002 = 3,750 N (Over 380 kg of force!).
⚠️ Common Trap & Error Prevention

Trap: Subtracting speeds as scalars: Δp = m(25 - 25) = 0.
Repair: Velocity is a vector! Direction reversal turns the minus sign into an addition: 25 - (-25) = 50 m/s.

Unit 5 of 6 · Relational Dynamics

Newton's Third Law Action-Reaction Pairs

F_{A→B} = -F_{B→A}  (Equal, Opposite, on DIFFERENT Bodies)
⚡ The Crucial Inferential Step

Every single force is a mutual interaction between TWO bodies. If Body A exerts force F on Body B, Body B simultaneously exerts an equal and opposite force -F on Body A. Action and reaction CANNOT cancel each other out because they act on TWO DIFFERENT bodies!

1
The 4 Invariant Criteria of Newton III Pairs 1. Equal in magnitude.
2. Opposite in direction.
3. Act simultaneously.
4. Act on two completely different bodies of the exact same physical origin (e.g. both gravitational, or both electromagnetic).
2
Internal vs. External Cancellation When both bodies are grouped into a single combined system (A + B), internal action-reaction pairs sum vectorially to zero, leaving system acceleration determined strictly by external forces.
💡 Classic Concept Check: The Book on the Table

Question: A book rests on a table. The normal reaction N pushes upward on the book, and gravity mg pulls downward on the book. Are N and mg a Newton's Third Law action-reaction pair?

Answer: NO! Absolutely not!
1. Both N and mg act on the SAME body (the book). Action-reaction pairs must act on DIFFERENT bodies.
2. They have completely different physical origins (N is contact electromagnetic repulsion between atomic electron clouds; mg is long-range gravitational pull of Earth).
• The true partner of upward normal N on the book is: Downward contact normal push on the TABLE.
• The true partner of downward gravity mg on the book is: Upward gravitational pull of the book on PLANET EARTH!
⚠️ Common Trap & Error Prevention

Trap: Confusing balanced forces on one body (Σ F = 0) with a Third Law interaction pair.
Repair: If two forces act on the same object, they are NEVER an action-reaction pair.

Unit 6 of 6 · Interfacial Thresholds

Static vs. Kinetic Friction & The Stick-Slip Threshold

f_s ≤ μ_s N  |  f_lim = μ_s N  |  f_k = μ_k N  (μ_k < μ_s)
⚡ The Crucial Inferential Step

Static friction is an adaptive, self-adjusting reaction force. It is NOT automatically equal to μ_s N! It matches the applied tangential force exactly (f_s = F_applied) up to the limiting threshold f_lim = μ_s N. The instant applied force exceeds f_lim, atomic cold welds shear, relative slip begins, and friction drops to the constant kinetic value f_k = μ_k N.

1
Static Regime (f_s ≤ μ_s N) System is at relative rest (a_rel = 0). Static friction adjusts to whatever value is required to maintain equilibrium: f_s = F_parallel.
2
Impending Motion Threshold f_max = μ_s N. On an inclined plane, impending slip occurs at the angle of repose: tan θ_repose = μ_s.
3
Kinetic Sliding Regime (F_applied > μ_s N) Surfaces slide relative to each other. Friction magnitude is constant: f_k = μ_k N, directed strictly opposite the relative velocity vector.
💡 Worked Problem: The 3-Stage Friction Test

Problem: A block of mass m = 10 kg sits on a horizontal floor with μ_s = 0.50 and μ_k = 0.40 (take g = 10 m/s²). Normal N = 100 N. Find the friction force and acceleration when pulled horizontally with: (a) F = 30 N, (b) F = 50 N, and (c) F = 70 N.

Solution:
• Threshold check: Limiting static friction f_lim = μ_s N = 0.50 × 100 = 50 N. Kinetic friction f_k = μ_k N = 0.40 × 100 = 40 N.
1. F = 30 N: Since 30 N < 50 N, block does NOT slip! Friction adjusts to match applied force: f_s = 30 N (NOT 50 N!), acceleration a = 0 m/s².
2. F = 50 N: Impending slip threshold. Friction reaches maximum: f_s = 50 N, acceleration a = 0 m/s².
3. F = 70 N: Since 70 N > 50 N, block SLIPS! Friction instantly drops to kinetic: f_k = 40 N. Net force = 70 - 40 = 30 N. Acceleration a = 30 / 10 = 3.00 m/s².
⚠️ Common Trap & Error Prevention

Trap: Setting f = μ_s N = 50 N when pulled with 30 N, concluding that the block accelerates backwards at 2 m/s²!
Repair: Friction is a passive reaction. It can never cause an object to move by itself; it only resists external applied forces up to its maximum threshold.
